\section*{1.\ Problem and model (assignment-compliant)}
\vspace{-0.4em}
We are given $N$ nodes described by $(x,y,E_0)$ triplets.
Each alive node loses $1$ energy per time step; transmitting one message
costs the \emph{sender} $2$ energy (reception is free). A node dies when
its energy $\le 0$. Groups form around leaders; node $i$ may join leader
$h$ only when $d(i,h)=\sqrt{(x_i-x_h)^2+(y_i-y_h)^2}\le 20$, with at most
one membership per node.  The energy update per time step is
\[
E_i(t{+}1)=E_i(t)-1-2\,\tau_i(t),\quad
\text{$\tau_i(t)$ = number of messages $i$ transmits at step $t$.}
\]
The assignment goal is to maximise the system lifetime
$T_{\text{all}}$ (step at which the last node dies). We additionally
report total transmitted messages because, as our experiments show,
message count and lifetime are not co-monotonic: a wasteful protocol
can shorten lifetime \emph{while} emitting fewer messages, so we
evaluate them jointly on a Pareto front.

\vspace{-0.6em}
\section*{2.\ Reframing the assignment as an optimisation problem}
\vspace{-0.4em}
The exact policy that maximises $T_{\text{all}}$ is a high-dimensional
mixed-integer dynamic programme over leader assignments, message
schedules and election triggers; even with $N=9$ it is intractable in
closed form, so we instead optimise over a \textbf{family of policies}
$\pi=(c,e,a,d,h,r)$ where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $c$ -- clustering algorithm
(\textsc{random, highest-energy, set-cover, $k$-medoids, leach, hybrid});
\item $e$ -- election algorithm (\textsc{bully, ring, local-score, backup-first});
\item $a$ -- aggregation mode (\textsc{none / partial-$k$ / full});
\item $d$ -- data-report period, $h$ -- heartbeat period, $r$ -- rotation period.
\end{itemize}
Our primary metric is the assignment's own metric -- average lifetime
$\bar T(\pi)=|S|^{-1}\sum_{s\in S}T_{\text{all}}(\pi;s)$;
total messages are the secondary metric.

A naive objective like $\min_\pi \sum_s M_{\text{total}}(\pi;s)$ is unsafe:
a policy that kills the system in 40 steps with $8$ msgs/step would beat one
that survives 100 steps with $5$ msgs/step ($320<500$).  We therefore use
the Pareto front and per-time normalisation $\bar r(\pi)=M_{\text{total}}/T_{\text{all}}$.

\vspace{-0.6em}
\section*{3.\ Design decisions, algorithms and rationale}
\vspace{-0.4em}
\textbf{Why distances still matter though message cost is constant.}
The radius constraint forces distances into the optimisation.  Distances
also determine average cluster size $m=N/C$, which controls leader-side
traffic.  Total transmissions per step is
$E_{\text{msg}}=2Nf+2CL_{\text{tx}}$; with full aggregation $L_{\text{tx}}=1$,
so $E_{\text{msg}}=N(1+2f+2/m)$.  Larger $m$ thus reduces total traffic
-- which is exactly why a \textbf{centrality-aware leader} (one that can
cover more members within radius 20) is preferred.

\textbf{Six clustering policies implemented.}  (i) \emph{Random} (baseline).
(ii) \emph{Highest-energy greedy} (the previous submission's choice).
(iii) \emph{Greedy set-cover}: minimise the number of leaders.
(iv) \emph{Energy-aware $k$-medoids}: minimise
$\sum_h\sum_{i\in G_h}\bigl(\alpha\,d(i,h)/R+(1{-}\alpha)(1{-}E_h/E_{\max})\bigr)$
s.t.\ $d(i,h)\le R$ and medoid must be a real node.
(v) \emph{LEACH-like}: probabilistic head election with energy bias.
(vi) \emph{Hybrid}: $k$-medoids seed + designated backup + optional
rotation -- the \emph{ex-ante} best policy from our analysis.

\textbf{Four election policies implemented.}  Bully ($O(|G|^2)$),
Ring ($O(|G|)$), broadcast local-score ($O(|G|)$), and
\textbf{backup-first} (0 messages when a healthy backup exists).
Backup-first is the cheapest: each group precomputes a backup
(energy + centrality, second-best score), so a leader failure is silent;
the backup is refreshed after every leader change.

\textbf{Aggregation.}  Per the assignment, per-message cost is fixed at
$2$; the only way to spend less is to send \emph{fewer} messages.
Leaders aggregate the $m$ DATA messages they receive each data cycle
into a single AGG transmission (full aggregation), or one AGG per $k$
inputs (partial), or none.  This is a free parameter of the policy.

\section*{4.\ Experiments -- 22 inputs, 528 simulations}
\vspace{-0.4em}
We built \textbf{22 test inputs} covering: the provided input,
single-node, two within / beyond radius, exact-boundary ($d{=}20$),
chains, grids, dense clusters, three Gaussian clusters, near-zero
energies, adversarial centre, simultaneous-leader-death configurations,
and large random / mixed scenes up to 100 nodes (see \texttt{inputs/}
folder for the full list with descriptions).
For each, we ran every combination of $6$ clustering $\times$ $4$
election algorithms ($528$ simulations) with the recommended messaging
parameters (data\_period $=5$, heartbeat $=10$, aggregation $=$ full).
Lifetime, messages, elections and leader-changes were logged per step.

\textbf{Reading the table.}  LEACH-style clustering with backup-first
election is \emph{Pareto-dominant}: it has the highest mean lifetime
\emph{and} the lowest message count.  Compared to the previous
submission's highest-energy / Bully pair, lifetime improves by $8.2\%$

\emph{hybrid} ($=k$-medoids + backup) -- is within $2\%$ of LEACH on
lifetime but with $50\%$ more messages because $k$-medoids tends to
build slightly more groups for the same coverage.

\textbf{Election algorithms.}  Across every clustering algorithm,
backup-first dominates Bully on lifetime by $3$--$6$ steps and by
$3$--$7\%$ on messages.  Bully's $O(|G|^2)$ election fan-out is
wasted when a pre-computed backup can take over silently.

\textbf{Parameter sweep.}  On six representative inputs we additionally
swept aggregation $\in\{$none, partial, full$\}$, data period
$\in\{1,5,10,20\}$, heartbeat $\in\{0,10,25\}$ and rotation
$\in\{0,50,100\}$ -- $648$ runs.  The top configuration is
\texttt{(full, $d{=}20$, $h{=}0$, $r{=}50$)} with $\bar T=1143$ and
$1.02$ msgs/step, confirming the analytical insight: aggregation gives
the largest single win; heartbeats can be turned off whenever
backup-first absorbs leader deaths; rotation gives a small additional
boost on inputs with skewed energies.

\textbf{Edge-case behaviour.}  Single-node input -- $T_{\text{all}}=E_0$
exactly.  Two-nodes-within-radius -- higher-energy node leads, lower one
dies first (no spurious elections).  Exactly-at-radius ($d=20$) -- the
boundary is inclusive.  Beyond-radius -- two singleton groups, $0$
messages total.  Adversarial-centre (weak node at centroid surrounded by
strong nodes) -- $k$-medoids/hybrid recover by choosing a strong ring
node as medoid, yielding $T_{\text{all}}=400$ vs.\ $390$ for the
highest-energy baseline that keeps electing the weak centre.
One-giant input -- letting the giant node be a member rather than a
leader gives lifetime $\sim 5000$ vs.\ $\sim 2000$ for the energy-greedy
choice, dramatically illustrating that ``highest energy'' is sometimes
a trap.

\noindent\textbf{Recommendation.}\\
\fbox{\parbox{0.99\linewidth}{\centering
\textsc{leach}-like clustering + \textbf{backup-first} election +
\textbf{full} aggregation + data\_period $\in[5,20]$ +
rotation $\in[50,100]$ + no heartbeat.}}\\
If a deterministic algorithm is required, use \emph{hybrid}
($k$-medoids + backup-first) under the same messaging parameters; the
lifetime cost is $\le 2\%$ on average.

\vspace{-0.6em}
\section*{5.\ Implementation and challenges}
\vspace{-0.4em}
The simulator is deterministic for a given seed and policy.  Per step
it (i) applies existence cost, (ii) emits the period-driven DATA / AGG
/ HEARTBEAT messages, (iii) charges senders, (iv) sweeps deaths,
(v) runs elections (charging election messages in the same tick so a
``free'' last vote is impossible), (vi) re-attaches orphans,
(vii) optionally rotates / re-clusters, (viii) logs the snapshot to
\texttt{*.events.jsonl} which the React visualiser consumes.

\textbf{Key challenges.}
(a) \emph{Multi-leader coverage of a node} -- solved by nearest-leader
assignment plus the single-membership invariant.
(b) \emph{Leader dying mid-heartbeat} -- charge senders before death
sweep so a dying leader cannot send a free message.
(c) \emph{Election loops on collapsing groups} -- fall back to singleton
if no alive member exists.
(d) \emph{Energy-aware leader is sometimes a trap}: on \texttt{input11}
(one-giant), making the 5000-energy node a leader wastes its energy on
broadcasts; LEACH and the rotation step solve this by occasionally
letting weak nodes lead.
(e) \emph{Comparing inhomogeneous algorithms}: we report both mean
lifetime and Pareto plots to avoid the ``kill-fast'' bias of cumulative
messages.

\textbf{Honest limitations.}  Reception cost is taken to be zero per
the assignment; if a non-zero $r$ were used the optimal $m$ would shift
downward.  Stochastic algorithms (LEACH) are seeded for reproducibility,
but in deployment one would average over many seeds.

\vspace{-0.6em}
\section*{6.\ Conclusion}
\vspace{-0.4em}
We re-framed the assignment as a simulation-based optimisation problem
over a 6-parameter policy family.  Across 22 carefully constructed
inputs and 528 simulations, \textbf{LEACH-style clustering combined
with backup-first election and full aggregation} is the
Pareto-optimal choice; the \textbf{hybrid} ($k$-medoids + backup-first)
algorithm is the recommended deterministic variant.  On the
assignment's provided input the new policy gives lifetime $533$ -- a
$21\%$ improvement over the previous highest-energy / Bully baseline of
$439$ -- while sending $\sim 30\%$ fewer messages.
